# Behavioral research notes and claim register

**Status:** Complete Phase 0 evidence baseline; no YourTurn outcome study or validated drift model.

**Owner:** Behavioral Scientist · **Task:** YT-005 · **Reviewed:** November 11, 2026

## How to use this register

Classify every statement as **sourced finding**, **product hypothesis**, or **normative safeguard**. A citation establishes what its source supports, not that YourTurn works. No invented effect sizes, universal friendship-hour prescriptions, or claims of proven drift prediction are allowed. Pitch claims must use the approved wording/scope below or undergo a new source review; unsupported behavioral claims are cut, not repaired with “research shows.”

The setup brief is the provenance of the eight ethical rules in [ETHICS_CHECKLIST.md](ETHICS_CHECKLIST.md), not research evidence. [PRINCIPLES.md](PRINCIPLES.md) applies the evidence while clearly separating policy choices.

## Sourced findings and limits

### R-01 — SDT's canonical psychological needs

**Source:** Richard M. Ryan and Edward L. Deci, “Self-Determination Theory and the Facilitation of Intrinsic Motivation, Social Development, and Well-Being,” _American Psychologist_ 55(1), 68–78 (2000); author-hosted paper, abstract and opening SDT section.[1]

**Type:** Theory/research synthesis, not a trial of YourTurn. The paper identifies competence, autonomy, and relatedness as three psychological needs.[1]

**Allowed pitch wording:** “Our design draws on Self-Determination Theory's needs for autonomy, competence, and relatedness.”[1] This states a theoretical design basis, not a demonstrated benefit.

**Limits:** This paper does not evaluate a rotating friendship fund, this notification system, or this graph. Fairness and belonging without exclusion are our additional design commitments, not two more canonical SDT needs. Do not claim SDT proves that payments, rotation, or reminders preserve friendships. These are limits on our interpretation, not findings of product effectiveness.

**Evidence locator:** Printed page 68, abstract and section “Self-Determination Theory.” Primary PDF fetched and text inspected; not sourced from a secondary marketing summary.

### R-02 — Time together and developing friendships

**Source:** Jeffrey A. Hall, “How many hours does it take to make a friend?”, _Journal of Social and Personal Relationships_ 36(4), 1278–1296 (2019 issue); first published online March 15, 2018; DOI `10.1177/0265407518761225`. Publisher paper retrieved as archived July 8, 2025.[3]

**Type and finding:** Two observational studies: recently relocated adults recalled time with a new acquaintance; first-year students reported on new acquaintances repeatedly. Time together was associated with friendship closeness, including changes over time in the student study.[3]

**Allowed pitch wording:** “Observational research on developing friendships associates time spent together with closeness.”[3]

**Limits:** Association is not proof that increasing scheduled time causes closeness. These were developing friendships, not an intervention in YourTurn's existing circles. Self-reported time and context limit transfer; the adult study discusses retrospective estimation and survivor-selection limitations.[3] Do not turn estimated hours into a target, threshold, obligation, prediction cutoff, or promise that a hangout repairs a relationship.

**Evidence locator:** Abstract; “Study 1: Adult geographic relocation and friendship”; “Study 2: Longitudinal friendship in first-year students”; general discussion. This archived primary publisher page supports the stated finding; it does not establish current product performance.

## Normative safeguards — not scientific outcome claims

Privacy of drift, no rankings/shame/dark patterns, affirmative revocable tracking consent, easy exit, hardship confidentiality, whole-group contribution agreement, the $15 cap, and claim sourcing are requirements chosen for this product. They do not need an invented experimental effect size to be mandatory. Their exact wording is preserved in the ethics checklist.

No real numeric scores may be visible. Q-002 keeps the red/green graph restricted to a clearly labeled synthetic-fixture demo until Privacy, Behavioral Scientist, and a human resolve the conflict. Any future permitted upcoming-host suggestions must be positive only. Turning tracking off excludes incident inferred edges, not just the person's direct records. Own-data access versus third-party inference access, hardship disclosures, retention/deletion, and forfeit legality remain owner decisions; this file grants no legal or privacy approval.

## Future research and claim gate

Before conducting real-user research, record the question, population, consent/withdrawal procedure, minimum necessary data, access/retention plan, potential harms, and stop conditions for Privacy and Behavioral review. Do not enroll someone merely because their host agreed. Participant refusal must not affect circle standing or trigger a warning to friends. Avoid collecting hardship reasons or publishing identifiable pair-level results.

For causal claims, require an appropriate study design rather than rebranding correlations. For predictive claims, require a defined outcome, independent held-out validation, missingness and subgroup/error analysis, and evaluation of disclosure/false-warning harms; no performance threshold is invented here. Small usability sessions can inform usability, not establish population-level efficacy. Record null/negative findings and study limits alongside positive results.

For each new claim, record: exact proposed wording; claim type; primary source; study population/design; supported finding; relevant limits; evidence locator; product transfer assumptions; reviewer and reviewed revision. Behavioral Scientist approves the evidence interpretation; Demo & Pitch maintains the actual claim inventory. Until approved, label internal hypotheses as hypotheses and omit unsupported external behavioral promises.
